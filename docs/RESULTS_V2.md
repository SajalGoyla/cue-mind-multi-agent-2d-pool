# CueMind v2: results summary (2026-11-26)

All numbers come from `results/summary.md` (generated by `python experiments/analyze.py` from `results/v2_pilot`, `results/v2_floor`, `results/v2_main`). Design: `docs/PLAN_V2.md`, decision D-029. Model for all LLM agents: Qwen 2.5 3B Instruct (Ollama, temperature 0.7, seeded); prompts frozen as `prompts-v2` before the main run.

## Question
Given the same shot menu and no simulated forecasts, does a team of specialised LLM agents (MA) play better eight-ball than a single LLM agent (SA), measured by full games against the same simulation-based reference player (Stat)?

## Main result (300 games, 30 paired seeds, seats swapped, common random numbers; 0 errors)

| Playing noise | Stat vs Stat: breaker wins | SA win rate vs Stat | MA win rate vs Stat | Paired MA − SA per seed (wins out of 2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **none** (primary; passed the pilot) | 43 % [27, 61] | **22 %** [13, 34] | **25 %** [16, 37] | **+0.07** [−0.17, +0.30]; MA better on 7 seeds, SA on 5, 18 ties; sign test p = 0.77 |
| **low** (secondary; failed the pilot) | 57 % [39, 73] | **37 %** [26, 49] | **33 %** [23, 46] | **−0.07** [−0.37, +0.23]; MA better on 8, SA on 9, 13 ties; p = 1.0 |

**Answer: no detectable difference.** The team is not better than the single agent in either condition; both intervals for the paired difference comfortably include zero. This was the expected outcome stated before the run (60 games per pairing resolve only large differences, about ±13 points).

Figure: `results/figures/v2_win_rates.png`.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| none | SA | 1.02 | 1,859 / 103 | 1.9 s | — |

MA costs about **3.9× the calls, 3.7× the input tokens, 6× the output tokens and ~4× the time per decision** of SA, for no measurable gain.

## Supporting steps

- **Pilot (bots only):** Stat beats the easiest-pot bot 77 % [65, 86] at zero playing noise (pass) but only 57 % [44, 68] at low noise (fail): with stroke errors, luck blurs the effect of decisions. Stat vs Stat shows no breaker advantage with the v2 menu (37 % / 43 %).
- **Floor check:** SA won 2/10 (none) and 3/10 (low) games against Stat, above the 10 % floor, so the comparison was measurable.
- **Prompt development (dev seeds only, 2 rounds, `results/prompt_iterations_v2.csv`):** restating the shot in each reason cut speed mix-ups from 12 % to 5 %; presenting the safety specialist as a "risk" specialist cut its safety-first nominations from 91 % to 51 % and raised MA unanimity from 0 % to 27 %.

## Interpretation (for the report)

1. With identical information, splitting the decision across specialists does not improve play for a 3B model; the coordinator must still integrate everything, and the extra stage costs ~4× compute.
2. Results do not depend on the noise condition: MA ≈ SA both when decisions clearly matter (zero noise) and when luck dominates (low noise).
3. Against Stat, both LLM systems do better at low noise (≈ 35 %) than at zero noise (≈ 23 %): stroke errors hurt the stronger, simulation-guided player more than they help it.

## Limitations

One small open model; 60 games per pairing (wide intervals); the menu's *selection* uses simulation (the agents never see forecasts); Stat has more information than the LLMs by design; no kicks or banks; simulated stroke noise rather than human play; no user study.
