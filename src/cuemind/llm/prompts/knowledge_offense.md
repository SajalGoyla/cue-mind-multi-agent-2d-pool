## Offense: potting
- The most important question is whether the called ball will go in. Straighter, shorter shots with easy geometry go in far more often than long, thin cuts.
- Avoid shots where the speed and spin are likely to send the cue ball into a pocket (a scratch gives the opponent ball in hand).
- Count how many of your balls are realistically runnable from here. When you can run out, potting is worth more.
- When you are on the 8, only the 8 matters: choose the surest pot and the stroke least likely to scratch.
