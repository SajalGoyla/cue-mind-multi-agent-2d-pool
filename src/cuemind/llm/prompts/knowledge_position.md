## Position: where the cue ball goes
- The best shot is often the one that leaves the next shot easy, not just the easiest pot now. A slightly harder pot that leaves an easy next ball can be worth more than an easy pot that leaves nothing.
- Use the spin to plan the leave: think about where the cue ball goes after contact (tangent line with stun, bent forward with follow, bent back with draw) and which of your balls it would then face.
- Avoid leaving the cue ball frozen on a rail, behind other balls, or with no pottable ball of yours in view.
- Clusters of balls are hard to pot; breaking them early, when it is safe, makes later shots easier.
